## Right now (early October)
Cotton in Punjab is **kharif**: sown ~April–May, **picked October–November**
([Pakistan crop calendar](https://irfarm.com/blogs/latest/seasonal-crop-calendar-pakistan-month-by-month-guide);
[calendar table](https://sbbsuniversity.ac.in/farmers%20app/crops%20punjabi.html)).
So in early October a Punjab cotton farmer is **picking**, still watching for
**whitefly / cotton leaf curl virus** and **pink bollworm** on late bolls, and
beginning to **prepare for wheat sowing** (the cotton→wheat rotation), which starts
in November. A 7-day pilot now should centre on **picking quality, late-season pest
scouting, and spray safety**, not sowing. *(confidence: high on season, medium on
the week-by-week emphasis — confirm with extension officer.)*

**Picking interval is contested:** one Pakistani commercial source says American
cotton every **15–20 days**, Desi every 8–10 ([GrowTech](https://www.growtechsol.com/cotton.html));
an Indian extension source says **every 7–8 days** ([Apni Kheti](https://www.apnikheti.com/en/pn/agriculture/crops/fibre-crops/cotton)).
Both agree: **pick when there is no dew, and don't delay — fallen cotton loses
quality.** Let the agronomist set the exact interval. *(confidence: medium)*

## B. Key seasonal decisions (small farmer)

| Decision | Practice (to verify) | Timing | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Variety | choose a CLCuV-tolerant approved variety | at sowing | medium — get current AARI approved list |
| Sowing date | within Apr–May window; avoid very late | spring | medium |
| Whitefly/virus mgmt | early sowing + balanced fertiliser + remove host weeds; control whitefly to cut CLCuV spread | whole season | **high** (well sourced, see C) |
| Scouting | weekly field scouting against ETL before any spray | squaring → boll | high (IPM principle) |
| Irrigation | avoid water stress at flowering & boll fill | Jul–Sep | medium |
| Fertiliser | per **soil test**; balanced N–P–K (over-N worsens CLCuV) | per schedule | medium |
| Picking | pick dry, at regular intervals, keep contamination out | Oct–Nov | medium |
| Spray safety | PPE; follow label pre-harvest interval | any spray | needs official source |

Over-use of nitrogen and unnecessary insecticide sprays are repeatedly linked to
**worse** whitefly/CLCuV outcomes and resistance; IPM (scout-then-spray on ETL)
cut sprays sharply without yield loss in field trials
([IPM reduces pesticide load](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Economic-Threshold-Levels-ETLs-of-major-insect-pests-of-cotton_tbl1_281188213)). *(confidence: medium–high)*

**CLCuV is the headline disease of Punjab cotton.** It is whitefly-transmitted, has
driven repeated epidemics since 1990 (Multan → Burewala → the recent Rajasthan
strain in central Punjab), and is associated with losses around **30%** in Pakistan
nationally. Management is **prevention**, because an infected plant cannot be cured —
which is exactly why whitefly control and clean fields matter so much. My sample
dictionary did not include CLCuV; **it should be the first disease added.**
*(confidence: high that CLCuV is dominant and prevention-based; the 30% figure is a
widely-repeated review number — present it as "~30%, reported", not as measured.)*

> On chemicals: Pakistani sources name imidacloprid, diafenthiuron, acetamiprid and

## E. Safety notes (mark all for official confirmation)
Wear covered clothing, gloves and a mask when spraying; don't spray into wind; keep
others and animals away; wash hands/face and change clothes after; store pesticide
in its labelled container away from food and children; observe the label's
**pre-harvest interval**; know poisoning signs (nausea, dizziness, excess salivation)
and seek medical help immediately. **Doses, intervals and first-aid specifics must
come from the product label and an official source — do not invent them.**

## 5 facts worth quoting (each with a source)
1. Punjab cotton is sown Apr–May and **picked Oct–Nov** (crop calendar).
2. **CLCuV is whitefly-transmitted and has no cure once a plant is infected** — management is prevention (CCRI Multan; critical review).
3. CLCuD has caused **repeated epidemics in Punjab since 1990** and is tied to ~30% reported losses (3rd-epidemic paper; review).
4. Whitefly ETL in Punjab Pakistan is reported around **4–5 adults/leaf**; jassid ~1–1.5/leaf (Pakistani study).
5. **IPM (scout-then-spray on ETL) cut insecticide sprays sharply without losing yield** in trials (ETL/IPM source).

## 3 things NOT to claim
1. Don't state **any pesticide dose** until an official Pakistani source is cited and an agronomist approves it.
2. Don't present the **30% loss** figure as your own measurement — it's a repeated review estimate.
3. Don't treat **Indian ETLs/varieties** as valid for Pakistan without confirmation.
